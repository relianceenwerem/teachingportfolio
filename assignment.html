Rehumanizing
Data.

A scaffolded project that moves students from understanding data humanism in theory to practicing it under real conditions.

Delivery context

By the time students reach this assignment they have worked with datafication, data humanism, and feminist ethics of care, and (in an earlier low-stakes exercise) built an artifact with Claude, then regenerated it using data-humanism principles. Now they turn to real digital-humanities projects to unpack how those projects center individual voices, choose care over extraction, and hold subjectivity.

Learning objectives

What students will be able to do.

  • i.  Explain data humanism and feminist ethics of care.
  • ii.  Identify and interpret data-humanistic principles in DH projects.
  • iii.  Articulate gaps between DH projects and other data-driven public communication.
  • iv.  Express rhetorical choices behind visualizations and evaluate how projects center or erase stories.
  • v.  Articulate the ethical responsibilities of data collectors, curators, and communicators.
  • vi.  Adapt the data-humanism framework to projects within their own field.
The scaffold

Two stages, one arc.

The assignment is built to move students through cognitive, affective, and psychomotor learning, from analytical fluency to original making. Open each step to see what it involves.

Stage 1 · Analytical fluency

a 7–10 page essay · due by spring break

  1. 1

    Students build a shared vocabulary through data humanism and feminist-data scholarship: Al-Hazwani et al., Drucker's Visualization and Interpretation and DH Coursebook, Lupi's Data Humanism, and the Feminist Data Manifest-No.

  2. 2

    Students identify a digital-humanities project and analyze how it exemplifies the heuristic of care, embodiment, contextualization, small data, and subjectivity, using anchor projects like Data by Design, the Middle Passage VR Experience, and Responsible Datasets in Context.

Stage 2 · Original making

group multimodal project · due at semester's end

  1. 3

    In small cross-disciplinary groups, students select 30–50 news articles reporting on a public-health risk, crisis, or disaster, one that leans heavily on data, statistics, or demographic figures.

  2. 4

    Groups read closely for how each article frames the people behind the data: where it centers stories and where it erases them.

  3. 5

    Students use AI to support textual analysis across the corpus, but AI is itself under study: they interrogate its output rather than accept it, making its role visible in the final work.

  4. 6

    Groups reconcile what close and distant reading each revealed, deciding what matters and why before they build.

  5. 7

    The core output: a blog, vlog, video, podcast, social post, or infographic that integrates argument with visual elements to dramatize the difference between datafied and humanized communication (see the two options below).

    Every group also submits a 1–2 page process document: how they moved from close reading through AI interrogation to a final product, tracing what they changed, what they struggled with, and what it taught them about their own relationship to data.

  6. 8

    Each group presents its project and receives structured peer feedback, closing the loop from private analysis to public, communal knowledge.

The lens students apply

The five-dimension heuristic.

Every stage of this assignment is read through the same five dimensions. Select one to revisit what it asks of a data project.

Care

Treating data as a relationship, not a resource: honoring an obligation to the people a dataset represents.

In this assignment

Students name the human cost of an article's choices before recommending how its data should have been handled.

Embodiment

Remembering that every data point was once a living body with a story numbers alone cannot hold.

In this assignment

The community-facing narrative restores the bodies a crisis statistic abstracts into a rate per 100,000.

Contextualization

Refusing the myth that numbers speak for themselves; situating data in the conditions that produced it.

In this assignment

Close reading of 30–50 articles surfaces the conditions a single headline figure leaves out.

Small data

Valuing the intimate, hand-scaled, and particular over the totalizing sweep of “big data.”

In this assignment

Annotated excerpts and hand-made comparative displays hold meaning that a dashboard would flatten.

Subjectivity

Making the interpreter visible; acknowledging that every visualization argues a position, for someone.

In this assignment

The process document makes the group's (and the AI's) interpretive choices explicit rather than hidden.

The core output

Two ways to rehumanize.

Option A

Policy brief

An evidence-based argument to decision-makers (public-health officials, NGOs, analysts, agencies) for how data about a crisis should be communicated differently. It names the original article's specific failures across the heuristic, articulates their human cost, and recommends concrete, actionable change grounded in data humanism.

Option B

Community-facing narrative

A rewrite of the article for the community the data (mis)represents, telling the same story with the same data, but in a way that restores embodiment, care, context, and subjectivity to the people inside the numbers.

Assessment

Weighted toward process.

Multimodal project40%
Participation in group activity15%
Reflective essay / process document15%
Presentation & peer dialogue15%

AI can help with distant reading, but the reflection and re-imagined narrative must be substantively the students' own. That is where their voice becomes visible.
